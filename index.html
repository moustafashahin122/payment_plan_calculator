∑ How it works (technical details)

The calculator uses Net Present Value (NPV) logic: the present value of all future payments, discounted at the chosen rate, equals the entered Cash Value.

Step 1 — Build a percentage cashflow

We construct a template cashflow in percentages. Position 0 is the down payment. Positions 1..N are equal installments. Custom payments are added on top of their respective positions.

cashflow[0]     = down_payment_pct × 100
cashflow[1..N]  = installment_pct × 100      where installment_pct = (1 − down − Σ custom) / N
cashflow[idx]  += custom_pct × 100           for each custom payment at index idx

Step 2 — Compute factor via NPV

We compute the Net Present Value of that cashflow, then divide the sum of cashflow by the NPV to get the factor (the value multiplier).

rate_per_period = annual_rate / payments_per_year
NPV(rate, cf)   = Σ  cf[t] / (1 + rate)^t       for t = 0..length(cf)-1
factor          = Σ cf  /  NPV(rate_per_period, cf)

Step 3 — Convert to amounts

Future Value (FV) = factor × Cash Value. Each individual payment is its percentage × FV.

FV          = factor × cash_value
down        = down_payment_pct × FV
installment = installment_pct  × FV
custom_i    = custom_pct_i     × FV          (added to its installment row)
total       = down + Σ installments + Σ customs

Step 4 — Round up (optional)

For customer-friendly amounts, each payment is rounded UP to the nearest 10ⁿ. This makes the displayed total slightly higher than the raw NPV-balanced total.

round_up(x, n) = ⌈ x / 10ⁿ ⌉ × 10ⁿ

Why this works

NPV guarantees that, at the chosen interest rate, the discounted sum of every scheduled payment equals today's Cash Value. This is the same math used in mortgage and lease contracts.
